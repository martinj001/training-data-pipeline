# Training Plan: November 24 – December 7, 2026

**Focus:** Bring running back, carefully. Two short easy runs a week, about 30 minutes
each, back in the Thu and Sat slots. Everything else is built to protect the left
knee while it gets used to running again. The last block took running off completely,
and the Nov 22 test run (3.7 mi, 45 min, avg HR 135) shows the knee can handle some.
This block finds out how much it handles *regularly*. The daily rolling and gentle
stretching stay. The hamstring bridge holds move up to single-leg, which loads the
tendon more without stretching it. Hinges (RDL, single-leg RDL, KB swings) stay out.
Upper body is mostly unchanged.

## Notes

- **Runs: ~30 min, easy, flat.** Thu and Sat. This is a time cap, not a distance goal.
  Keep a pace where you can talk in full sentences. As a guide, keep avg HR around
  your Nov 22 run (~135–145), not the ~160 of a normal run. Flat routes or soft
  surfaces (trail, grass, track) are better than hills: downhills and steep climbs
  put the most load on the back of the knee.
- **During-run rule:** a mild ache at the back of the knee up to 3/10 is OK if it
  stays steady or fades. If it climbs during the run, or gets sharper than 3/10, walk
  the rest of the way. That still counts as a finished session.
- **Next-morning rule (the getting-up check):** the morning after each run, notice how
  the knee feels when you stand up after sitting. Same as usual or better = keep
  going. Worse = make the next run a ride. Worse after two runs in a row = back to
  all-bike and book the physio.
- **Week 2 stays at ~30 min,** even if Week 1 felt great. Holding the same amount for a
  full block before adding more is how tendons adapt. The next block can go to ~40 min
  if this one is clean.
- **Return-to-running check (still the gauge):** you're starting runs ahead of the
  full 4-point check, so treat it as a running scorecard. Try each one sometime this
  block:
  1. Getting up after sitting feels normal (or very close) 5 mornings in a row
  2. Single-leg hamstring bridge hold, left leg, 45 sec at ≤2/10, no worse next day
     (this is built into Week 2 of Session B)
  3. 30-min brisk walk with no knee symptoms
  4. 10 small single-leg hops on the left with no pain
- **Physio:** if the knee isn't clearly better by Dec 7, or runs keep making mornings
  worse, book the sports physio. Go sooner if anything new shows up: swelling, the knee
  catching or giving way, or swelling/tenderness in the calf.
- **Pain rule for strength work (0–10 scale):** up to 3/10 at the knee during an
  exercise is OK if it's back to normal by next morning. Sharper than that, or worse
  the next morning, means drop that exercise next session.
- **Session B is the day after a run.** The bridge holds and squats come ~24 h after
  Thursday's run. If the knee is grumpy Friday morning, skip the squats and split
  squats that day and still do the bridge holds, calf work, and lateral walks.
- **Thanksgiving (Thu Nov 26):** if the day is busy, move the run to Wed and do the
  quality ride another day (or skip it). The run matters more than the trainer session
  this week.
- **Wednesdays: climbing or a trainer ride, your pick.** FTS MTB is done until spring,
  so climbing takes its place as the Wednesday option (climbing on Nov 25). Climbing
  and the knee: **heel hooks are the one move to skip or keep light.** Pulling with
  the heel bends the knee against resistance, which is exactly the medial hamstring
  tendon that's irritated. High steps and deep drop-knees are fine if they're ≤3/10,
  same pain rule as strength. **Downclimb or lower off; don't jump off the wall**
  (bouldering especially): a hard landing is a big, sudden knee-bend load, like
  jumping off the bike on MTB. Climbing also works the upper body, so if forearms or
  shoulders are cooked Wednesday, Tuesday's Session A the next week can stay as
  written. Just don't add load that week.
- **Play the weekend by ear.** The second run can be Sat or Sun, whichever fits.
  Do the ride or MTB on the other day. Group rides and MTB always trump the plan. A
  Sunday run gives the knee an extra rest day after Session B, so pick that when you
  can. On MTB, still go
  easy on hike-a-bike and jumping off the bike.
- **Hollow holds are 30 sec now.** The plan said 18 sec, but you've been holding 30.
  The plan now matches what you actually do.

## Running Support Routine

**Before (3 min, dynamic, no static holds): every run**
- Leg swings, front-to-back and side-to-side, 10 each, each leg (easy range, don't
  reach)
- Walking lunges, 6
- Easy high-knees march in place
- 2–3 min brisk walk before you start jogging

**After (5 min): every run**
- Hamstring strap stretch, 2 × 20 sec per side, *gentle* (bend the knee slightly if
  it tugs at the back of the knee)
- Chair hip hinge, 2 × 30 sec per side
- Calf stretch, straight knee then bent knee, 20 sec each per side

On run days this after-run stretch can stand in for the Daily Hamstring Care Routine.
Still do the Daily Knee Rolling.

## Daily Knee Rolling: do right before the Daily Hamstring Care Routine

**~5 min, daily.** Foam roller or lacrosse ball, moderate pressure (around 5/10,
"good hurt," never sharp). 60–90 sec per area, slow passes, pause on tight spots.

1. **Inner hamstrings:** sit on the roller, turn the leg so the inner back thigh is on
   it, and roll from just below the glute down to **a hand-width above the knee
   crease. Stop there.**
2. **Adductors (inner thigh):** lie face down with the left leg out to the side, knee
   bent, roller under the inner thigh, and roll from groin to knee. (Some of these
   muscles attach right next to the sore spot.)
3. **Calves:** both heads, from ankle to just below the knee crease.
4. **Glutes:** sit on the ball/roller and cross the ankle over the opposite knee.

**Don't roll directly in the back-of-knee crease.** Nerves and blood vessels run right
through there, and pressing straight on an irritated tendon usually stirs it up. Work
the muscles above and below it instead.

**Keep the stretches gentle:** you want mild tension in the muscle, not a pull at the
sore spot. If a stretch tugs right at the back of the knee, bend that knee slightly
until it doesn't.

## Daily Hamstring Care Routine

**5 min, daily. Best when warm: evening, or after a walk/shower.** This is a standing
daily practice, not tied to this block. Carry it forward into future plans until the
hamstring/behind-knee discomfort has fully resolved.

1. Supine hamstring strap stretch (lying on back): 2 × 20 sec per side
2. Chair hip hinge: heel on a low chair, hinge from the hips with a flat back,
   2 × 30 sec per side
3. Calf stretch, straight knee then bent knee: 20 sec each, per side (the calf
   crosses behind the knee too, so don't skip it)
4. Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch: 30 sec per side (tight hip flexors tilt the
   pelvis and keep the hamstrings in tension all day)

**Yoga flow alternative:** on days a short guided flow feels better than isolated
holds:
- [Gentle Hamstring & Calf Stretch Yoga](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JLEyt_n_oXk):
  gentle, no half-splits. Closest match to the hamstring + calf focus here, good while
  the discomfort is still active.
- [20 Min Yoga For Tight Hamstrings and Hips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xcjpoxfaJ0w):
  same areas plus hips/hip flexors, a bit more active. Better once things have calmed
  down some.

## Strength Sessions

### Session A — Upper (~55 min)

**Warm-up (5 min): keep it pain-free, not a test**
- Arm circles, band pull-aparts (light), 10 scapular push-ups, hip flexor stretch.

**KB row (single arm)**
- 3 × 10 @ 35 lbs, holding (RPE 7, clean). If it stays RPE 7 both weeks, try 3 × 12
  next block.

**DB Overhead Press**
- 3 × 9 @ 50 lbs/hand, a small step up. 3 × 8 came in at RPE 7.7 on Nov 17, so add
  one rep per set. If the last set grinds past RPE 9, go back to 8s.

**Pull-ups**
- 3 × 8 @ bodyweight, holding (RPE ~9).

**Push-ups, full superset**
- 3 × 15 standard + 3 × 12 incline, holding.

**Standing Calf Raise, bodyweight only, no bar**
- 3 × 15 @ bodyweight, 2-sec up, pause at top, 2-sec down. These help the run come
  back too.

**Core finisher** (hanging knee raises → hollow hold → plank, done as one round)
- 3 rounds: 9 hanging knee raises, 30 sec hollow hold
- 1 round push-up to downward dog
- 2 rounds: 70 sec plank (posterior tilt, tuck pelvis)

### Session B — Lower / Knee-Friendly (~55 min)

**Pain rule applies to every exercise here:** ≤3/10 at the knee during, back to normal
by next morning. Otherwise drop that exercise next session. If the knee is grumpy
from Thursday's run, skip the squats and split squats and still do the rest.

**Warm-up (7 min): gentle, no big swings**
- Hip circles, small-range leg swings front/side, 10 glute bridges, 10 banded
  clamshells, 30 sec 90/90 hip switch each side, light banded hamstring curls × 10

**Squat**
- 3 × 10 @ 135 / 155 / 155 lbs, holding (that's what you logged on Nov 13, RPE 8.7).
  Stop if the back of the knee gets sharp at the bottom.

**Hamstring Bridge Hold (the main tendon work), now single-leg**
- Lie on your back with your heels on a bench/chair and knees bent about 90°. Push
  heels down, lift hips, and *hold still*. Both-leg holds were RPE 4 on Nov 13, so
  it's time to move up.
- **Week 1:** 4 × 30 sec **single-leg**, each side (left is the one that matters; do
  right to match). 1 min rest between.
- **Week 2:** 4 × 45 sec single-leg, each side, if Week 1 was ≤3/10 and the next
  morning wasn't worse. A left-leg 45 sec at ≤2/10 checks off item 2 of the
  return-to-running check. If Week 1 was borderline, repeat 30 sec.

**KB Glute Bridge**
- 3 × 12 @ 35 lbs, 1-sec squeeze at the top. (Logged RPE 4.7. If it's still easy,
  hold the KB at the top for 2 sec.)

**Bulgarian split squat / Calf raises superset**
- 3 rounds: split squats × 10 each leg @ 40 lbs → immediately calf raises × 15,
  rest 90 sec

**Running-support work**
- Single-Leg Calf Raise: 2 × 12 each leg @ bodyweight, slow and controlled
- Banded Lateral Walk (monster walk): 2 × 15 steps each direction @ light band
  (glute medius, helps the knee track straight when you run)

**Core finisher** (hanging knee raises → hollow hold → plank, done as one round)
- 3 rounds: 9 hanging knee raises, 30 sec hollow hold, 70 sec plank (posterior tilt)

## Trainer tempo — rotating options (Wednesday rides)

On Wednesdays that aren't climbing days, this is the one quality ride. Pick whichever
option fits the day. If riding outdoors instead, no structure required. Keep it to Option 3 or
skip it if recovery is in the red. The runs are the priority this block.

**Option 1 — Sweet Spot**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 2 × 15 min sweet spot (top of Z3 / bottom of Z4), 5 min Z2 recovery between
- 10 min cooldown

**Option 2 — Over-Unders**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 3 × 10 min alternating 2 min over-threshold (Z4) / 2 min under (Z3), 5 min Z2
  recovery between sets
- 10 min cooldown

**Option 3 — Classic Z3 tempo (fallback / lower-key day)**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 4 × 8 min Z3 ("comfortably hard": you can talk, but wouldn't want to), 5 min Z2
  recovery between
- 5 min cooldown
