# Training Plan: November 10 – November 23, 2026

**Focus:** Calm down the irritated tendon at the back-inside of the left knee. No
running for the whole block — every run slot becomes a ride. The pattern (stiff
when standing up after sitting, loosens with walking but never fully clears, mild
ache through a run) reads like an irritated medial hamstring tendon, most likely
semimembranosus/semitendinosus where it attaches behind the inner knee. Not a
diagnosis — if it isn't clearly better by Nov 23, book a sports physio. Three things
work together this block: (1) take running off it completely, (2) daily rolling plus
*gentle* stretching for the tight muscles around it, and (3) gradual, pain-free
loading of the tendon itself — the part research supports most for tendon
irritation, since tendons get calmer when they're loaded, not when they're only
rested and stretched. Upper body unchanged.

## Notes

- **No running Nov 10–23.** The ~3 mi capped runs Nov 3 and 5 were done slowly and
  completed, but the knee didn't get better — so this block removes running entirely
  rather than capping it again. Thu and Sat cardio slots become easy rides. Walking
  is fine and encouraged (it's the thing that loosens the knee up).
- **The daily check — getting up after sitting.** That stiffness is the best gauge
  for how the tendon is doing. Notice it each morning: better, same, or worse than
  yesterday. Trending better = the plan is working. Worse two mornings in a row =
  back off whatever was new the day before.
- **Pain rule for strength work (0–10 scale):** up to 3/10 at the knee during an
  exercise is OK. It must settle back to normal by the next morning (use the
  getting-up check). Sharper than 3/10, or worse next morning → drop that exercise
  for the next session, don't push through it.
- **Session B swaps out the hinges.** On Oct 30 the RDL and single-leg RDL both
  aggravated the back of the knee, so they're off this block — hip hinges under load
  stretch the hamstring tendon while loading it, which is the hardest combo for an
  irritated tendon. KB swings go too (same hinge, done fast). In their place:
  - **Hamstring bridge hold** (isometric — holding still, no movement) — loads the
    tendon without stretching it. Week 1 both legs, Week 2 left leg on its own if
    Week 1 felt fine.
  - **KB glute bridge** — keeps the glutes and posterior chain working.
  - Squats, split squats, calf raises, and lateral walks stay (they all logged fine).
- **Bike check:** riding should feel fine on this. If the knee starts complaining on
  the bike, check saddle height first — too low means lots of knee bend under load,
  too high means the hamstring reaches at the bottom of every pedal stroke.
- **Group rides and MTB always trump the plan.** Sat and Sun can swap — both are
  cardio slots. On MTB, take it easy on hike-a-bike and jumping off the bike — big,
  sudden knee-bend moves are the riskiest part of a ride for this.
- **Return-to-running check (end of block, Nov 23)** — all four should be true before
  the next block starts a walk-run:
  1. Getting up after sitting feels normal (or very close) 5 mornings in a row
  2. Single-leg hamstring bridge hold, left leg, 45 sec at ≤2/10, not worse next day
  3. 30-min brisk walk with no knee symptoms
  4. 10 small single-leg hops on the left with no pain
- **Not improving by Nov 23 → sports physio.** Earlier if anything new shows up:
  swelling, the knee catching or giving way, or swelling/tenderness in the calf.

## Daily Knee Rolling (new) — do right before the Daily Hamstring Care Routine

**~5 min, daily.** Foam roller or lacrosse ball, moderate pressure (around 5/10 —
"good hurt," never sharp). 60–90 sec per area, slow passes, pause on tight spots.

1. **Inner hamstrings** — sit on the roller, turn the leg so the inner back thigh
   is on it, roll from just below the glute down to **a hand-width above the knee
   crease. Stop there.**
2. **Adductors (inner thigh)** — lie face down, left leg out to the side with knee
   bent, roller under the inner thigh, roll groin-to-knee. (Some of these muscles
   attach right next to the sore spot.)
3. **Calves** — both heads, ankle to just below the knee crease.
4. **Glutes** — sit on the ball/roller, cross the ankle over the opposite knee.

**Don't roll directly in the back-of-knee crease.** Nerves and blood vessels run
right through there, and pressing straight on an irritated tendon usually stirs it
up. Work the muscles above and below it instead.

**Keep the stretches gentle this block:** mild tension in the muscle, not a pull at
the sore spot itself. If a stretch tugs right at the back of the knee, bend that knee
slightly until it doesn't. Hard stretching can irritate a sore tendon more.

## Daily Hamstring Care Routine

**5 min, daily — best when warm: evening, or after a walk/shower.** This is a
standing daily practice, not tied to this block — carry it forward into future plans
until the hamstring/behind-knee discomfort has fully resolved.

1. Supine hamstring strap stretch (lying on back) — 2 × 20 sec per side
2. Chair hip hinge — heel on a low chair, hinge from the hips with a flat back —
   2 × 30 sec per side
3. Calf stretch, straight knee then bent knee — 20 sec each, per side (the calf
   crosses behind the knee too — don't skip it)
4. Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch — 30 sec per side (tight hip flexors tilt the
   pelvis and keep the hamstrings in tension all day)

**Yoga flow alternative** — on days a short guided flow feels better than isolated
holds:
- [Gentle Hamstring & Calf Stretch Yoga](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JLEyt_n_oXk) —
  gentle, no half-splits; closest match to the hamstring + calf focus here, good while
  the discomfort is still active.
- [20 Min Yoga For Tight Hamstrings and Hips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xcjpoxfaJ0w) —
  same areas plus hips/hip flexors, a bit more active; better once things have calmed
  down some.

### Session B — Lower / Knee-Friendly (~55 min)

**Pain rule applies to every exercise here:** ≤3/10 at the knee during, back to
normal by next morning. Otherwise drop that exercise next session.

**Warm-up (7 min) — gentle, no big swings**
- Hip circles, small-range leg swings front/side (easy, not reaching for range),
  10 glute bridges, 10 banded clamshells, 30 sec 90/90 hip switch each side, light
  banded hamstring curls × 10

**Squat**
- 3 × 10 @ 115 / 135 / 155 lbs — holding. Logged fine on Oct 30 even with the knee
  bothering you. Stop if the back of the knee gets sharp at the bottom.

**Hamstring Bridge Hold (new — the main tendon work)**
- Lie on your back, heels on a bench/chair, knees bent about 90°. Push heels down,
  lift hips, and *hold still*. You should feel the hamstrings working hard; the knee
  itself should be ≤3/10.
- **Week 1:** 4 × 30 sec, both legs, 1 min rest between.
- **Week 2:** if Week 1 felt fine and the next mornings weren't worse — 4 × 30 sec
  **single-leg**, each side (left is the one that matters; do right to match). If
  Week 1 was borderline, repeat Week 1 instead.

**KB Glute Bridge (replaces KB swing)**
- 3 × 12 @ 35 lbs — KB on the hips, feet on the floor, 1-sec squeeze at the top.

**Bulgarian split squat / Calf raises superset**
- 3 rounds: split squats × 10 each leg @ 40 lbs → immediately calf raises × 15,
  rest 90 sec

**Running-support work — kept (minus single-leg RDL)**
- Single-Leg Calf Raise — 2 × 12 each leg @ bodyweight, slow and controlled
- Banded Lateral Walk (monster walk) — 2 × 15 steps each direction @ light band
  (glute medius — helps the knee track straight)

**Core finisher** (hanging knee raises → hollow hold → plank, done as one round)
- 3 rounds: 9 hanging knee raises, 18 sec hollow hold, 70 sec plank (posterior tilt)

## Trainer tempo — rotating options (Wednesday rides)

Thu and Sat rides are **easy Z2** — no structure, just spin. Wednesday is the one
quality ride; pick whichever option fits the day. If riding outdoors instead (or FTS
MTB is on), no structure required.

**Option 1 — Sweet Spot**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 2 × 15 min sweet spot (top of Z3 / bottom of Z4), 5 min Z2 recovery between
- 10 min cooldown

**Option 2 — Over-Unders**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 3 × 10 min alternating 2 min over-threshold (Z4) / 2 min under (Z3), 5 min Z2
  recovery between sets
- 10 min cooldown

**Option 3 — Classic Z3 tempo (fallback / lower-key day)**
- 10 min easy warm-up (Z2)
- 4 × 8 min Z3 ("comfortably hard" — can talk, wouldn't want to), 5 min Z2 recovery
  between
- 5 min cooldown
